Every key in Enclave Vaults carries a `KeyPolicy` that controls **who** can do **what**, **under which attestation conditions**, and **with which approvals**. The policy is part of the sealed state of the vault and is enforced inside the enclave on every call. There is no admin override.

A policy has four parts: `principals` (who is recognised), `operations` (what each may do, and on which conditions), `mutability` (which parts of the policy may change later, and by whom), and `lifecycle` (how long the key lives, see [Key lifecycle](/solutions/enclave-vaults/key-lifecycle)).

## Principals

The vault recognises two kinds of principal.

| Principal | Authentication | Matched on |
|---|---|---|
| **OIDC** | A bearer JWT, verified against the vault's configured issuer and audience through JWKS | `issuer`, and `sub`, and the token's roles against `required_roles`. An empty `sub` matches by role alone |
| **TEE** | Mutual RA-TLS: the caller's SGX or TDX quote is read from its certificate and verified through an attestation server | an [attestation profile](#attestation-profiles) |

The policy names them in four slots:

| Slot | Kind | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `owner` | OIDC, required | the person or service that owns the key |
| `managers` | OIDC, any number | people who approve sensitive operations or new measurements |
| `auditors` | OIDC, any number | people who read the key's audit log |
| `tees` | TEE, any number | the enclaves allowed to use the key |

A principal that is not listed cannot influence the key in any way. Rules refer to them as `Owner`, `Manager(i)`, `Auditor(i)`, `Tee(i)` or `AnyTee`.

Live human approval does not need a principal of its own: it comes through the identity provider, which issues a short-lived token after a wallet or passkey ceremony (see [Step-up](#step-up)).

## Operations

These operations are governed by the policy: `Sign`, `Wrap`, `Unwrap`, `Mac`, `ExportKey`, `DeleteKey`, `UpdatePolicy` and `PromoteProfile`. Creating a key needs a creation grant from the platform; listing keys, reading the audit log, staging and revoking a pending profile, and issuing approval tokens follow fixed rules described below.

Each rule has the form "these operations are allowed for these principals when these conditions hold":

```json
{
  "ops": ["Sign"],
  "principals": [{ "Tee": 0 }],
  "requires": [
    { "ManagerApproval": { "manager": 0, "fresh_for_seconds": 300 } },
    { "TimeWindow": { "not_before": 0, "not_after": 1798761600 } }
  ]
}
```

An operation is allowed when **any** rule lists the operation and the caller, and **all** of that rule's conditions pass.

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `AttestationMatches(profile)` | The caller's RA-TLS certificate satisfies `profile`, in addition to the principal match. |
| `ManagerApproval { manager, fresh_for_seconds }` | The request carries a valid [approval token](#approval-tokens) from manager number `manager`, no older than `fresh_for_seconds`. |
| `TimeWindow { not_before, not_after }` | The vault's trusted time is inside the window, in Unix seconds; `0` leaves that side open. |
| `OidcStepUp { required_amr, operation_bound, fresh_for_seconds, binding }` | The caller's token proves a fresh step-up, bound to this operation; see below. |

### Step-up

`OidcStepUp` requires the caller's OIDC token to carry every method in `required_amr` (for example `["webauthn"]`) and to have been issued within `fresh_for_seconds`. The identity provider issues such a token only after a wallet or passkey ceremony. The token is also bound to this exact operation through its `vault_op` claim, so an approval for one operation cannot be replayed for another. `binding` says which binding the key takes, and the key accepts only that one.

**Binding 2**, the canonical condition `{"required_amr":["webauthn"],"operation_bound":true,"fresh_for_seconds":300,"binding":2}`, covers every operation that governs a key: `create` (of a re-created name, see [Deleted names](#deleted-names)), `promote`, `export`, `update_policy`, `update_policy_from`, `delete` and `delete_after`. The approval binds:

- the members of the constellation (each vault checks that it is named);
- the operation and the key handle;
- the target: the measurement being promoted, the digest of the new policy, or the successor of a key being deleted after a move;
- the policy version, so any change to the policy invalidates outstanding approvals;
- the approver, a nonce and an expiry.

The token must be fresh within 300 seconds, its expiry is at most 10 minutes ahead (24 hours for a `delete_after`, which is presented only once the move has finished), and the vault accepts each approval once. Keys created before binding 2 keep binding 1 (`operation_bound` with `binding` absent), which binds the handle, the measurement, the policy version, a nonce and an expiry for promote and export.

### Approval by default

A new key's policy must ask for a wallet approval (a passkey step-up bound to the operation) on every rule through which a person can promote, update or delete the key, and export it when it is exportable. A policy without one is refused unless it opts out explicitly with `"approval": "none"`. Rules that name only enclaves (`Tee`, `AnyTee`) are exempt: an app fetching its own key is not a governance act. A later update cannot take away the approvals a key has.

Exporting a key you created through the platform always requires an operation-bound WebAuthn step-up no older than 300 seconds.

## Attestation profiles

An attestation profile describes what a calling enclave must look like.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `name` | A label for the profile. |
| `measurements` | The accepted measurements: `Mrenclave` (SGX, hex) or `Tdx { mrtd, rtmr1, rtmr2 }` (all three must match). Any one listed measurement is enough. |
| `attestation_servers` | The attestation servers that may verify the caller's quote. At least one. |
| `required_oids` | `(oid, expected_value)` pairs that must appear in the caller's RA-TLS certificate, such as the app's code hash. |
| `acceptable_tcb_statuses` | The platform TCB statuses accepted from the attestation server. A `Revoked` platform always fails. |

A policy usually lists several measurements or profiles (for example, the version running now and the next one) so that operations continue across deploys.

## Approving a new measurement

When the app that uses a key is rebuilt, its measurement changes, and the new build has no access to the key until someone allowed to promote it approves. The new measurement waits in a **pending** slot:

1. **Stage.** The owner, a manager, or the platform stages the new profile (up to 16 pending per key). Staging grants nothing.
2. **Promote.** A caller allowed `PromoteProfile` by the policy approves it, typically with a step-up. The profile joins the key's TEE principals and the policy version increases. A promotion only adds: `required_oids` can be added or strengthened, never dropped.
3. **Revoke.** A pending profile can be withdrawn by whoever the policy's mutability allows; the platform cannot revoke.

A key can opt in to `auto_migrate_to_next_attestation_profile`. Only then, and only for a profile the platform staged whose change is to the runtime alone (same required OIDs, attestation servers, TCB policy and TEE family), is the promotion automatic. Only the owner can turn this on or off. Retiring an old measurement once the new one runs is a separate step.

## Approval tokens

For a `ManagerApproval` condition, a manager of the key asks the vault for an approval token naming a key handle and an operation. The vault issues it as a JWT it signs itself, after checking that the caller is that manager.

- A token lives 5 minutes by default, and at most 1 hour.
- It is bound to the key handle, the operation, the manager's index, the approver's subject and the **policy version** when it was given. Any change to the policy invalidates every outstanding token, so an approval given before a change is never accepted after it.
- When a manager slot is matched by role rather than by a named subject, the approver must be a different person from the one making the request (co-signing).

A manager compromised on their own cannot use a token outside its lifetime or for another operation. A caller compromised on its own cannot mint one.

## Mutability

`mutability` says which parts of the policy may change after creation, and by whom. It lists fields under `owner_can`, `manager_can` and `immutable`, from `Owner`, `Managers`, `Auditors`, `Tees`, `Operations`, `Lifecycle`, `Mutability` and `PendingProfiles`.

- A field in `immutable` never changes for the life of the key.
- Auditors and TEE principals can never change the policy.
- Only the owner can switch automatic migration on or off.
- A key's expiry date never moves, whatever the policy allows; see [Key lifecycle](/solutions/enclave-vaults/key-lifecycle).
- The owner can always tighten a key: adding the canonical bound passkey step-up to existing rules that people use (and that govern promote, update, delete or export) is allowed even where `owner_can` does not list `Operations`, unless `Operations` is `immutable`. Nothing else may change in the same update.
- A rule change on a binding 2 key sends the new policy exactly as `CanonicalisePolicy` returns it (the form the vault stores and returns), so the owner approves the same JSON the vault keeps. As `update_policy_from`, the approval also binds the digest of the rules it replaces, so the wallet can show both.

## Deleted names

Deleting a key leaves a tombstone: the material is gone, and the vault remembers the name until the key's original expiry. Every way of creating a key stops at a tombstone, so a deleted name cannot quietly come back with a new value. Re-creating it is an explicit act: a new handle ending in `new-<k>`, created only with the owner's bound `create` approval of the exact policy it will hold. When the original expiry passes, the tombstone and the key's audit log are removed and the name is free again.

## How a typical call is evaluated

For a sensitive `Sign` call by an application enclave:

1. The enclave opens a mutual RA-TLS channel to the vault holding the key. The vault verifies its quote and matches it against the key's TEE principals.
2. The vault finds a rule that allows `Sign` for that principal.
3. It checks each of the rule's conditions: for example a manager approval token (signature, lifetime, handle, operation, policy version) and a time window.
4. It signs inside the enclave and returns the signature. The private key never leaves.
5. The call, allowed or denied, is appended to the key's audit log.

## Audit log

Every operation on a key, allowed or denied, is recorded in that key's audit log with a sequence number, the time, the operation, the caller and the outcome, plus a reason for a refusal. Any principal of the key can read it (`privasys vault key audit`). The log is sealed with the vault's storage.

## Why this is more than RBAC plus attestation

Classical RBAC says "this user can do this thing". The vault adds two orthogonal axes:

- **The calling code's identity must match.** Not just the user, but the exact enclave binary acting on their behalf. This removes "a secret leaked from a CI runner" as a threat.
- **A fresh act of human consent can be required.** Not a long-lived token, but a per-operation wallet or passkey ceremony. This removes "a compromised long-lived credential" as a threat for the operations that matter most.
