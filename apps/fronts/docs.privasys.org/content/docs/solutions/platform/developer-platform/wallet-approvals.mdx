---
title: Wallet approvals
description: How an app asks a person to approve one act on their phone, and how it checks the answer before committing.
---

Some acts need more than a signed-in session: a decision with legal effect,
granting a role, erasing personal data. What matters is that a named person
approved **this act, now**, not that they signed in strongly this morning.

A wallet approval gives your app exactly that. Your server asks the person's
Privasys Wallet to approve one act. The wallet shows it, the person approves
or declines with their phone's biometric, and your server receives a short-lived
token bound to that act. Your server checks the token before it commits.

Approvals work for any app deployed on the platform. The person needs a
Privasys ID, linked to their account in your app.

## The flow

1. Your server sends the approval to the person's wallet, through the platform.
2. The wallet checks that your app sent it: it connects to your app's own
   platform hostname over [RA-TLS](/solutions/enclave-os/attestation/ra-tls/),
   verifies the attestation and the app id in it, and asks your app to confirm
   it is waiting on this exact approval.
3. The wallet shows your app's registered name, the act and the assurance you
   asked for. The person taps **Approve** or **Decline**.
4. The wallet asks for a fresh biometric, then signs with the person's
   Privasys ID passkey. Privasys ID verifies the signature, including user
   verification, and issues an approval token.
5. The wallet posts the token to your app, over RA-TLS, to the same host.
6. Your server checks the token and commits the act.

## Address the right person

An approval is addressed to the person's Privasys ID subject under your app's
sign-in client. That is the `sub` your app sees when they sign in with their
Privasys ID.

If people sign in to your app another way (Microsoft, Google), link their
Privasys ID once with the Auth SDK's `linkIdentity`, and store the subject it
returns against their account in your app. A person can link more than one
Privasys ID. If they remove your app in their wallet, approvals stop reaching
them.

## Send an approval

Call the platform's notify endpoint with your app's attested identity, the
same call as any wallet notification:

```http
POST https://api.developer.privasys.org/api/v1/notify
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "sub": "<the person's Privasys ID subject>",
  "type": "approval",
  "payload": { "approval": "<the approval, as a JSON string>" }
}
```

The approval itself is a JSON string, so its digest is unambiguous:

```json
{
  "v": 1,
  "id": "dec_7f3a",
  "title": "Grant the auditor role to A. Lovelace",
  "lines": [
    { "label": "Role", "value": "Auditor" },
    { "label": "Granted by", "value": "B. Turing" }
  ],
  "act_digest": "<lowercase hex SHA-256 of the act>",
  "assurance": "device",
  "callback": "/api/approvals/answer",
  "ttl": 300
}
```

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `id` | Your reference, 1 to 64 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`. |
| `title` | What the person approves, up to 120 characters. |
| `lines` | Up to 12 labelled lines: label up to 40 characters, value up to 200. |
| `act_digest` | The SHA-256 your server computes over its own canonical form of the act. The wallet never computes it; your server recomputes it before committing. |
| `assurance` | `device` or `gov-presence` (see below). |
| `callback` | A path on your app's host where the wallet delivers the answer. |
| `ttl` | How long the approval waits, 60 to 900 seconds. |

Text is plain: no markup, links, control characters, bidirectional overrides
or zero-width characters. The wallet shows your app's registered name; you
never supply it.

Keep the exact approval string. Its SHA-256 (lowercase hex) is the
`approval_digest` the answer commits to.

## Serve two routes

On your app's platform hostname (`<app>.apps.privasys.org`), which the wallet
reaches over RA-TLS:

- `GET /.well-known/privasys/approval?id=<id>&digest=<approval_digest>`
  answers `200 {"open": true, "id": "<id>", "approval_digest": "<digest>"}`
  while the approval waits, and `404` otherwise. The wallet shows no buttons
  until your app confirms.
- `POST <callback>` receives `{"approval_id", "decision", "token"}`.

## Check the token

The token is a JWT with header `typ: op-approval+jwt` and `alg: ES256`,
signed by Privasys ID ([JWKS](https://privasys.id/jwks)). It lives five minutes.

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| `iss` | `https://privasys.id` |
| `aud` | `approval:<your app id>` |
| `exp` | In the future. |
| `approval_id`, `approval_digest` | The approval you sent, and the SHA-256 of its exact string. |
| `act_digest` | Equal to the digest you recompute from the act you are about to commit. |
| `sub` | The Privasys ID linked to the person acting. |
| `decision` | `approve` to commit. `decline` is a signed refusal: record it. |
| `acr` | At least the assurance the act needs. |
| `amr` | Contains `webauthn`; also `face` for `gov-presence`. |
| `jti` | Not seen before. Each token is used once. |
| `v` | `1`. |

Keep the token with the act: it is your evidence that the person approved it.

## Assurance levels

| Level | The person | The token proves |
|---|---|---|
| `device` | Uses Face ID or their fingerprint, prompted afresh for this approval. | The holder of this Privasys ID approved this act on their phone, with user verification. |
| `gov-presence` | Takes a live selfie, matched in the identity-verifier enclave to the passport photo they verified in their wallet. | The person whose passport was verified approved this act in person. The verifier's signed result is in the token's `presence` claim, for your records. |

`gov-presence` is coming soon. Until then, an approval that asks for it is
refused with a clear error when you send it.
