By default, a Microsoft sign-in on a Privasys app goes through the Privasys app registration. An organisation can bring its own instead: your staff then authenticate through an application registered in your tenant, under your consent policy and your Conditional Access rules.

You do this once per sign-in client. It takes about twenty minutes and needs:

- the role to register applications and grant tenant-wide admin consent (Cloud Application Administrator or higher);
- the [CLI](/solutions/cli), signed in as an owner of the account holding the sign-in client;
- the sign-in client's id, shown when the client was created.

<Callout type="info">
The app's developers change nothing. The gate and the app's code stay as they are; only the registration behind the Microsoft button changes.
</Callout>

## 1. Create the app registration

In the [Entra admin centre](https://entra.microsoft.com), open **Identity > Applications > App registrations > New registration**.

- **Name**: what your users will see on the consent and sign-in pages.
- **Supported account types**: this decides who can sign in.

| Choice | Who can sign in through your registration | Tenant value below |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounts in this organizational directory only (single tenant) | Members of your tenant. People with Microsoft accounts elsewhere are refused by Entra and sign in with a Privasys ID passkey instead. | your tenant id |
| Accounts in any organizational directory (multi-tenant) | Any work or school account, through your registration. | `common` |

- **Redirect URI**: platform **Web**, value `https://privasys.id/auth/social/callback`.

Note the **Application (client) ID** and the **Directory (tenant) ID** from the overview page.

## 2. Register the federation and get the certificate

The preferred credential is a certificate. Privasys ID generates the key pair inside its host's key management service, where the private key stays; each sign-in's token request is signed there, and nothing is shared with you or with anyone else.

```bash
privasys oauth-clients federation set <sign-in-client-id> microsoft \
  --tenant <directory-tenant-id> \
  --client-id <application-client-id> \
  --display-name "Your Organisation" \
  --certificate
```

The command prints the public certificate and its expiry. Save the certificate as `privasys-id.pem`.

`--display-name` is the name on the sign-in button: "Sign in with Your Organisation".

## 3. Upload the certificate

In your app registration, open **Certificates & secrets > Certificates > Upload certificate** and choose `privasys-id.pem`.

If your policy requires a client secret instead, create one under **Client secrets** and pass it on standard input, so it never reaches a command line or an email:

```bash
privasys oauth-clients federation set <sign-in-client-id> microsoft \
  --tenant <directory-tenant-id> \
  --client-id <application-client-id> \
  --display-name "Your Organisation" \
  --client-secret-stdin < secret.txt
```

It is encrypted before it is stored, decrypted only inside a token exchange, and never returned by any listing. A secret needs you to rotate it by hand, which is why the certificate comes first.

## 4. Grant the API permissions

Under **API permissions**, add these Microsoft Graph **delegated** permissions: `openid`, `email`, `profile` and `User.Read`. Then choose **Grant admin consent for your organisation**, so your staff never see a consent prompt.

## 5. Check it works

Open the app and sign in. The button now reads "Sign in with Your Organisation" and takes you to your tenant's sign-in page.

```bash
privasys oauth-clients federation show <sign-in-client-id>
```

lists the registration, its tenant and when its certificate expires.

Your sign-in logs in Entra show the application by the name you gave it. To go back to the Privasys registration at any moment:

```bash
privasys oauth-clients federation remove <sign-in-client-id> microsoft
```

## Entra controls that work unchanged

Everything Entra applies to an application applies here, since the sign-in is yours:

- **Conditional Access and MFA**: target the application by name.
- **Who may sign in**: under **Enterprise applications**, set **Assignment required** and assign the users and groups allowed to reach the app.

## Renewing the certificate

The certificate lasts two years. Before it expires, run the `set` command from step 2 again. It generates a new key pair and prints the new certificate, and **the old key keeps signing every sign-in in the meantime**. Upload the new certificate alongside the old one, then:

```bash
privasys oauth-clients federation confirm-rotation <sign-in-client-id> microsoft
```

The new key starts signing and the old one is destroyed. You can then remove the old certificate from the registration.

## Your users stay the same people

A Microsoft account is identified by its directory object id, the `id` Microsoft Graph returns. That id belongs to the person in their directory, whichever app registration signed them in. So someone who used the Privasys registration before you brought your own keeps their account, and the app keeps seeing the same subject.

This holds for members of your tenant, and for anyone signing in to a multi-tenant registration from their own tenant. One case differs: someone you invited to your tenant as a **guest** has a separate object in your directory, with its own id. Signing in to a single-tenant registration as that guest is a different account from the one they have under their home tenant. If you already have people on the platform who will come back as guests, tell the app's owner before you switch, so their access can be granted again.

## What Privasys ID receives

Your staff authenticate with you. Privasys ID receives their directory id, name and email from Microsoft Graph to complete the sign-in, passes what the app has registered as required in the sign-in token, and keeps none of it: the identity provider stores no user profile.
